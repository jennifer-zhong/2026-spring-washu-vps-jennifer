When do you cherish the slow or hard way of doing something? Why?
When do you embrace constraints or prefer inefficiency? When do you willingly sacrifice convenience or comfort? What does the limited, slower, or more effortful approach offer that the quicker and easier way does not? What can we learn from this about human fulfillment and the kinds of technologies that might support it?

I prefer the slow or “hard” way of taking notes during class. A lot of the time, especially in heavily lecture based classes, it’s easier to just read through slides or take notes by typing on a laptop or even using AI. However, I like the feeling of physically writing notes by hand either pencil on paper or even with my Apple pencil on an iPad. Even though it takes longer and is “harder,” I think this style of notetaking allows me to fully process everything I’m learning in class and actually helps me retain the information better than if I was just typing notes or reading off of slides. I feel like even though it’s slower and more effortful, it improves my learning. I don’t think human fulfillment is just about doing things the most efficient way possible, but about doing things that are purposeful, meaningful, and allow us to slow down to actually think. I think now with AI and other technologies, it’s hard to actually learn material from classes when there are so many ways to just get the answers online, but I feel like actually writing notes is helping me in this situation.
