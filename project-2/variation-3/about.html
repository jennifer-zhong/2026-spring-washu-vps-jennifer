What is a Super Normal object?

A Super Normal object is something so ordinary and well-resolved that we stop noticing it as "designed" at all. It quietly performs, feels natural in the hand, and becomes part of daily life through clarity, usefulness, and familiarity.

About This Project

This site explores the idea of Super Normal through a visual archive of everyday tools, household forms, and industrial products. Instead of celebrating novelty, the project focuses on objects that have endured because their proportions, materials, and use patterns are already "right enough." The drawings, index pages, and photo studies are meant to slow down our attention and make these familiar forms visible again.

The project is built around close looking: comparing silhouettes, surface details, and handling cues that make an object understandable at a glance. By collecting these examples in one place, the site asks how good design can be humble, stable, and quietly memorable.

About The Book

Super Normal: Sensations of the Ordinary by Jasper Morrison and Naoto Fukasawa argues that many of the best objects are not dramatic statements, but things that disappear into life through consistent, thoughtful function. The book highlights objects across furniture, kitchenware, tools, and public equipment that feel inevitable rather than expressive.

Its central idea is that normality is not a lack of design. It is often the result of countless micro-decisions that reduce friction between people and objects. Over time, these pieces earn trust and become standards, not because they are loud, but because they are deeply usable.

Why It Matters

In a culture that often rewards constant visual novelty, Super Normal reminds us that design quality can be measured by durability, legibility, and care for everyday routines. A cup, stool, basket, or brush can carry intelligence without demanding attention.

Looking at these objects as a collection also reveals shared design values across regions and decades: balanced geometry, honest materials, and forms that support repeated use. This perspective shifts design from trend to long-term relationship.
